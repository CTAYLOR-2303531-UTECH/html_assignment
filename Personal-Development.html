When I initially saw this 'self help book' titled “The Richest Man in Babylon” by George S Clason. I scrunched my nose in annoyance. However, after reading it, similarly to a sponge I quickly absorbed it. I was pleasantly surprised by the gems that was bestowed upon me. I enjoyed it so much that I finished the entire book. The chapters that I will reflect on are "Seven Curses for a Lean Purse" and "The Five Laws of Gold" respectively The story follows Arkad, the richest man in Babylon, who explains how he accumulated his wealth despite starting as a poor scribe. Through a series of lessons and stories, Arkad shares timeless financial principles that anyone can apply.

Seven Curses for a Lean Purse

The chapter begins with dropping the first financial literacy gem "Start fattening your purse" The author further explains that one should keep at least a tenth of everything earned. He emphasizes setting spending limits through budgeting, profiting from your savings, seeking mentorship from financially literate individuals, working towards owning a home, preparing for retirement and constantly increase one's ability to own. I haven't always been the best with managing my money. If I see something and I have the means- I will get it, I believe in denying myself nothing. Arkad's story opened my eyes that being a spend rift is not the way. Yes initial gratification is great but especially since I am getting older I have to start thinking long term into the future. Saving responsibly and intentionally, maintaining a strict budget, preparing for the future and ultimately increase my earning capacity is the way to go. When I was in the US I saw so many elderly persons working at Walmarts, at the gas stations, restaurants etc. It really came as a culture shock to me because in Jamaica once persons are of a certain age they simply retire. It greatly piqued my curiosity and so I asked a lady who I was familiar with, "Why are you still working at your age?". My question took the her by surprise but she replied, "I made some really bad choices in my youth, I simply didn't prepare at all. At this rate I will be working up until I die". While reading the book, like a replayed video player, the scene recounted in my mind and at that, moment I decided that her reality would never be my reality. Since then, I have began to adapt some od these principles in my life since. I opened a Scotia investment account (my savings will earn for me), I have taken out life insurance (I pay a small contribution every month for emergencies) and I have made myself a budget and have become more deliberate in how I spend, what I spend on and where I spend.

The Five Laws of Gold

Secondly, In the chapter 'Five Laws of Gold' Algamish teaches his student Nomasir that wealth follows rules. It provided a great reinforcement on the knowlege points I learnt in previous chapters. The importance of saving steadily, being cautious with my spending, and be smart (don't get scammed). My greatest takeaway is that wealth is built by habits and not by chance. Through discipline and wisdom I will be able to not only consistently generate more wealth but also protect it. I need to curb all my urges to spend unnecessarily and be more diligent and focused. I should think long term, life after university and beyond that. Although I close mindedly began this book I want to say thank you for the recommendation it was a truly valuable read.
