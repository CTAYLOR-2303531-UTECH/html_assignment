LLMs and Me

“LLMs such as ChatGPT, Claude, and CoPilot must be used as a tool not a crutch". As a current university student, that specializes in a STEM centered degree, I whole heartedly agree. These Language Learning Models (LLMs) should be used as mere assistants to help students excel. My fear as a student is leaving university and not knowing how to do anything in my career field because I was heavily reliant on AI, therefore how it aids me and the academic danger it causes will be thoroughly discussed.

Firstly, Kasneci et al. (2023), states that AI is useful in creating a personalised learning space for student users. By generating personalised practice materials it adapts to the student's particular learning style, which improves the overall learning experience. I rely on these LLMS to generate practice questions on difficult topics for me. It tailors to my needs. For instance, when it comes on to calculations, my LLM knows that my personal style is a clear step by step approach very simplified with the formulas highlighted in red to help me to recall.

Secondly , A real academic danger of using LLMs is cognitive offloading. Roy & Jony (2026) defines this as transferring intellectual tasks to the AI rather than doing it yourself. It fosters a sense of laziness and it is quite appealing to choose option of lightening my work load once all my deadlines begin to steadily approach. This can do harm to my learning and shelves my critical thinking skills. Instead, I ask it to explain the concept so that I can do it myself and provide some advice after I have attempted to see how best I can improve.

To conclude, I believe that LLMs have a space and a role to play in this generation's education. Instead of over reliance it should be used as a tool to improve the learning process for students by explaining concepts, generating practice questions, provide feedback but most notably to continue learning beyond the classroom. Though convienient it should not be a means for cognitive offloading.
